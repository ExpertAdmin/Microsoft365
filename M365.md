# Connect Your Own Domain to Microsoft 365

**A simple step-by-step guide**

## Introduction

When you start using **Microsoft 365**, one of the first things to do is add your own domain (for example, `sajalroy.com`). This lets you create email addresses like `name@sajalroy.com`.

You do this in the **Microsoft 365 Admin Center**. It is the place where you add, check, and manage your domains.

<img width="1672" height="643" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d2818fba-e814-44e9-bcb8-63407e157ef9" />

In this guide, you will learn how to:
- Add your domain to Microsoft 365
- Prove that you own the domain (verification)
- Add DNS records
- Create a test user and give them a license
- Set up and test email

---

## Step 1: Sign in to the Microsoft 365 Admin Center

1. Go to https://admin.microsoft.com
2. Sign in with an **administrator account**.

---

## Step 2: Open the Domains page

1. In the menu on the left, click **Settings**.
2. Click **Domains**.

<img width="1365" height="601" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1a8d453e-a0d5-403f-af90-6735e31550d4" />

## Step 3: Add your domain

1. Click **+ Add domain**.

<img width="1365" height="601" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/18fd6ad2-4100-43e1-90ea-b1c2c6665071" />

2. Type your domain name (for example, **sajalroy.com**).
3. Click **Use this domain**.

<img width="1365" height="607" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/415105df-16fa-4936-b6ca-f1f8e74c6246" />

4. Select **Add your own DNS records**.
5. Click **Continue**.

<img width="1365" height="601" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8f99ccbf-5786-4ef9-a905-3256355f0f26" />

6. Microsoft will show you a **TXT record**. **Do not click Verify yet.** First, add this record in your domain hosting account (see Step 4).

<img width="1365" height="598" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/48d9a3fe-641f-42b2-b6a9-b412565a44cb" />

## Step 4: Add the TXT record in your domain hosting account

Your domain is managed by a hosting company. In this guide, my hosting company is **HOSTSEBA**. Your hosting company may look different, but the steps are very similar.

1. Sign in to your domain hosting account.

<img width="1351" height="600" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/6e1b2d5e-3176-47bd-9662-05514cc47d46" />

2. Click **Domains**.

<img width="1350" height="490" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/94cd2214-8b7f-42a5-b3b2-ad599b6c3219" />

3. Click **Manage**.

<img width="1354" height="406" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/bb230c30-a6eb-4e21-b019-ccfaff8808f7" />

4. Click **Manage DNS**.

<img width="1354" height="507" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fe45e0c2-a484-461c-a965-a4a194b1fc40" />

5. Click **Add New Record**.

<img width="1365" height="330" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/486762c4-ae7b-44ba-89b1-63326af1293d" />

6. Enter the following TXT record, then save it:

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Type | TXT |
| Name / Host | @ |
| TTL | 3600 |
| TXT Value | `MS=ms45493404` |

> **Note:** The TXT value above is only an example. Use the exact value that Microsoft 365 shows you.

<img width="1333" height="506" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/5893f9fa-cecd-4a31-90b0-d431efbe94a3" />

7. The record is now added.

<img width="1365" height="367" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b861837e-8b9c-49d9-bad7-dfeaca5f7de7" />

## Step 5: Verify your domain

1. Go back to the **Microsoft 365 Admin Center**.
2. Click **Verify**.

<img width="1365" height="604" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f82ab0bb-934e-406d-8539-49dff04df7d1" />

3. Wait while Microsoft checks the record.

<img width="1365" height="604" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8d3e518c-8531-42c6-94fe-278088920ea4" />

4. Click **Continue**.

<img width="1365" height="597" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/23df796d-579c-496e-97c9-90a20fef4202" />

5. Next, Microsoft asks which services you want to use with this domain.
   - If you want **email**, select **Exchange and Exchange Online Protection**. Microsoft will then give you more DNS records, including an **MX record**. You must add them to your domain's DNS settings.
   - **In this step, I am not setting up email.** I will do it later in this guide.

6. Click **Continue**.

<img width="1365" height="603" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/892e5d05-5a8f-44d2-bc1a-52a772a214bd" />

7. Click **Done**.

Your new domain is now added to the Microsoft 365 Admin Center.

## Step 9: Turn on email (Exchange and Exchange Online Protection)

1. In the Microsoft 365 Admin Center, go to **Settings** > **Domains**.
2. Select the domain you want to use for email. In this example, it is **sajalroy.com**.

<img width="1365" height="607" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/802543ec-5a9a-446c-ba3e-04356b21d627" />

3. Click **DNS records** > **Manage DNS**.

<img width="1360" height="567" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c0cf3141-c676-4cc1-b570-8acfe122eca3" />

4. Select **Exchange and Exchange Online Protection**.
5. Copy the **MX, CNAME, and TXT records** that Microsoft shows you.
6. **Do not click Continue yet.**

<img width="1365" height="1326" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/742d3f5a-a3c2-4064-b6bf-b40cc7559124" />

7. Add the **MX, CNAME, and TXT records** in your domain hosting DNS settings.

<img width="590" height="232" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/957fcb38-eadb-4f81-8a8f-fdd11426a6d7" />

8. Sign in to your domain hosting account and add the records there.

## Conclusion

Connecting your own domain to Microsoft 365 is easy if you follow these steps:

1. Add your domain in the Admin Center.
2. Prove you own it by adding a TXT record.
3. Add the email DNS records (MX, CNAME, and TXT).
4. Create users and give them licenses.
5. Test sending and receiving email.

After this, your own domain is ready to use with Microsoft 365.
